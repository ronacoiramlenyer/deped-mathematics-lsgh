### 2. Course Philosophy

The course introduces students to the fundamental data analytics workflow, from understanding and preparing data to analyzing results, communicating insights, and evaluating the quality of analytical work.

The emphasis is on:
- Data literacy and analytical reasoning
- Data quality, accuracy, and responsible data handling
- Practical application of statistical concepts
- Evidence-based interpretation and decision-making
- Clear communication through visualizations and dashboards
- Collaboration, peer evaluation, and constructive critique

The course should provide a meaningful foundation for further study in data analytics and data science without unnecessarily overwhelming students with advanced programming.

### 3. Primary Technology Stack

**Google Sheets → Google Colab (Python/pandas) → Looker Studio**

| Platform | Primary Purpose |
|---|---|
| Google Sheets | Data organization, validation, cleaning, spreadsheet functions, summaries, descriptive statistics, and introductory statistical analysis |
| Google Colab | Basic Python-based data preparation using pandas |
| Looker Studio | Data visualization, interactive dashboards, and communication of findings |
| jamovi or JASP | Optional demonstration of statistical analysis software for research-oriented work |

Google tools are the primary platforms because of accessibility, familiarity, and reduced setup requirements. Excel and Power BI may be acknowledged as industry alternatives but are not the core instructional tools.

### 5. Unit Specifications

#### Unit 1 — Foundations of Data Analytics (8 hours)
- Meaning, purpose, and applications of data analytics
- Data types, structures, and sources
- The data analytics workflow
- Data privacy, ethics, and responsible use
- Understanding analytical questions and evidence

**Illustrative activity:** Data Detective — students examine a real-world scenario and determine what data is needed, what questions can be investigated, and what limitations or ethical concerns should be considered.

#### Unit 2 — Data Collection and Management (10 hours)
- Data sources and collection methods
- Organizing datasets
- Data validation and consistency
- Basic file management, importing, and exporting
- Data privacy, integrity, and documentation

**Illustrative activity:** Dataset Stewardship — students inspect a dataset, evaluate its suitability for a given analytical question, identify data-management issues, and prepare it for processing.

#### Unit 3 — Spreadsheet Processing and Analysis (14 hours)
- Sorting, filtering, and organizing data
- Data cleaning and error detection
- Conditional and lookup functions
- Aggregation and summary statistics
- Pivot tables and comparisons
- Identifying patterns and trends

**Illustrative activity:** Spreadsheet Investigation — students clean and analyze a supplied dataset containing inconsistent entries, missing values, or duplicate records, then produce a summary supported by spreadsheet calculations.

#### Unit 4 — Python for Data Preparation (10 hours)
- Introduction to Google Colab and pandas
- Loading CSV files and examining DataFrames
- Selecting and dropping columns
- Identifying and removing duplicates
- Handling missing values
- Filtering, grouping, and summarizing data
- Exporting cleaned datasets

**Illustrative activity:** Python Cleaning Challenge — students use a prepared notebook and a permitted Python reference sheet to clean a dataset and export the result for subsequent analysis.

Python should remain deliberately accessible. Students are not expected to master object-oriented programming, advanced algorithms, machine learning, or extensive software development.

#### Unit 5 — Statistical Data Analysis (14 hours)
- Descriptive statistics
- Appropriate statistical summaries
- Correlation and regression, where prescribed by the official CG
- Hypothesis testing and statistical significance, where prescribed by the official CG
- Interpreting results and limitations
- Drawing evidence-based conclusions

**Illustrative activity:** Statistical Investigation — students examine a case, select suitable analytical methods, calculate or obtain results using the designated tool, and explain what the findings do and do not establish.

The exact statistical procedures and their depth must follow the verified DepEd competencies rather than assumptions about what the course should contain.

#### Unit 6 — Data Visualization and Storytelling (12 hours)
- Selecting suitable visual representations
- Creating clear charts and graphs
- Designing interactive dashboards
- Using filters and interactive elements
- Identifying meaningful patterns
- Communicating findings and recommendations
- Avoiding misleading visualizations

**Illustrative activity:** Dashboard Challenge — students transform an analyzed dataset into an interactive Looker Studio dashboard and prepare a concise, evidence-based explanation of its major findings.

#### Unit 7 — Culminating Analytics Project and Peer Review (12 hours)
- Applying the complete analytics workflow
- Preparing and documenting data
- Analyzing results
- Developing dashboards
- Evaluating another group's work
- Presenting evidence-based critiques
- Responding to feedback and identifying improvements

**Culminating activity:** Independent Peer Verification — each group completes its own analytics project, then evaluates another group's work. The group presents its critique, citing evidence of strengths, weaknesses, data-quality issues, analytical limitations, and recommended improvements. The reviewed group responds to the critique.

The final presentation focuses on the **critical review of another group's project**, not simply presenting one's own dashboard.

### 6. Case Study Design

Use several realistic cases throughout the course rather than relying on a single dataset or artificial exercises.

Possible case contexts include:
- School attendance and participation
- Retail sales and inventory
- Community surveys and local concerns
- Student or consumer preferences
- Environmental observations
- Publicly available datasets

Cases should provide a reason to analyze the data. Each should include a question or decision to investigate, relevant data, identifiable analytical challenges, and an output that supports interpretation.

Use static datasets as the default. Real-time streaming, automated ETL systems, and live sensor pipelines are outside the core course scope.

### 7. Practical Assessment Specifications

Practical examinations should be integrated throughout the course.

Assess students on:

1. **Execution:** Ability to perform the required task using the appropriate tool.
2. **Accuracy:** Correctness of data processing, calculations, and visualizations.
3. **Interpretation:** Ability to explain findings, limitations, and decisions.
4. **Documentation:** Evidence of the process and resulting output.
5. **Communication:** Ability to present conclusions clearly and responsibly.

For Python assessments, a teacher-provided reference sheet containing approximately 10–20 essential commands may be permitted. The assessment should prioritize appropriate application and interpretation over memorization of syntax.
